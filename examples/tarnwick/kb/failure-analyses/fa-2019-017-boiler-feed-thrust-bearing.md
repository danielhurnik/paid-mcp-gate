# FA-2019-017: Repeated thrust-bearing failures on a boiler feed pump

> Fictional sample content for the paid-mcp-gate demo. Not engineering advice.

## Situation

A multistage boiler feed pump at a combined-cycle power plant lost its thrust bearing three times in 14 months. Each time, the site replaced the bearing, found nothing wrong with the lubrication, and returned the pump to service.

## Evidence

- The babbitt on the active thrust pads was wiped; the inactive pads were undamaged. The bearing was overloaded in one direction.
- Oil analysis was clean, and oil temperatures were normal until hours before each failure.
- The **balance line leak-off flow** had risen by about 60% over the 18 months before the first failure. It was measured, but not trended or alarmed.
- On teardown, the radial clearance between the balance drum and its bushing was 2.4 times the design value, with erosion marks.
- Plant records showed a condenser tube leak and a period of poor feedwater chemistry two years earlier.

## Root cause

In a multistage pump, the balance drum carries most of the axial thrust produced by the impellers, and the thrust bearing takes only the residual. As particles eroded the drum clearance, more water leaked past the drum, the balancing force fell, and the residual thrust on the bearing grew until the babbitt failed. Replacing the bearing alone did not change the thrust, so the failure repeated.

## Corrective actions

1. Restored the balance drum and bushing clearances to the design value.
2. Added a leak-off flow alarm at 30% above the post-overhaul baseline.
3. Added an axial position probe with alarm and trip settings.
4. Tightened the feedwater chemistry monitoring procedure after condenser leaks.

## Outcome

No thrust-bearing failures in the five years since. The leak-off trend is now reviewed monthly and has flagged one early drum wear case at a sister unit.

## Lesson

Treat balance line leak-off flow as a leading indicator of thrust-bearing load. A rising trend warns you months before the bearing does.
