# FA-2021-042: Premature impeller wear on a tailings slurry pump

> Fictional sample content for the paid-mcp-gate demo. Not engineering advice.

## Situation

After a concentrator expansion, impeller life on the tailings pumps of a copper mine dropped from about 4,000 operating hours to about 1,500. The site had already tried two harder impeller materials without improvement.

## Evidence

- Wear was concentrated at the impeller eye and on the front shroud, with gouging on the suction-side liner (throatbush).
- A second pump had been added in parallel during the expansion. Flow measurements showed that each pump now ran at 50–55% of its best efficiency point (BEP) flow.
- The gap between the impeller and the throatbush had not been adjusted since the pumps were commissioned; measured gaps were three to four times the recommended setting.

## Root cause

Two effects combined. Operating far to the left of BEP created recirculation at the impeller eye, which concentrated wear there. At the same time, the growing front gap let slurry recirculate from the discharge back to the suction across the front shroud, which multiplied the wear. Harder materials could not fix a flow problem.

## Corrective actions

1. Reduced pump speed through a sheave change so that each pump runs at 80–95% of BEP flow in normal operation.
2. Introduced a weekly impeller-to-throatbush gap adjustment, with the measured gap logged in the maintenance system.
3. Returned to the original impeller material, which was cheaper and adequate once the flow was right.

## Outcome

Impeller life recovered to about 3,600 hours within six months. Wear parts spending on the tailings pumps fell by roughly 55%.

## Lesson

When a plant change alters the system curve, re-check where every pump now operates. Material upgrades are not a substitute for operating near BEP.
